Prologue
Scene 1 · Long Island · Five Years Ago
Before Everything Changed

Jonah Johnson grew up in a small house on Long Island knowing two things for certain: his mother worked herself to the bone, and money was always just out of reach. When the acceptance letter from SUNY Oneonta arrived, they cried at the kitchen table together. He was the first in their family to go to college.

He was a soccer player, a business major, a kid who genuinely liked studying. He had plans. His mother had hope for him — which is the same thing when you don't have much else.

Mrs. Johnson
"You go up there, Jonah. You make something of yourself. Don't let anything stop you. Promise me."

He promised. He meant it completely. But nobody warns you how fast a promise slips through your hands when the tuition bill arrives and the financial aid doesn't.

Scene 2 · SUNY Oneonta · Freshman Fall
The Weight of the Bill

Jonah's first semester was everything he'd imagined — early morning soccer practices, late nights in the library, professors who actually pushed him to think. His roommate, Jake Simone, was easy to live with and quick to laugh. Jake had grown up with a stable family and a comfortable house. He had never once stared at a tuition bill and felt sick.

By October, Jonah had taken every on-campus job available. Tutoring. Dining hall. Library shelving. He ran the numbers every Sunday night. Still not enough.

The gap between what he earned and what he owed grew every week. By November, Jonah had started spending time with Marcus Webb — a local guy who seemed to have money, a business brain, and no interest in questions.

Scene 3 · Off Campus · October
The Offer

Marcus had a way of making things sound simple. You're smart, you're clean, you've got a student ID — nobody suspects a college kid. Just be the middleman. Just hold the package, pass it on. Easy money. And Jonah needed money badly enough that "easy" sounded true.

Marcus Webb
"You in or you out, college boy? I don't do halfway. You work with me, you're protected. You don't, you're on your own."
Jonah
"I'm in. But I do this clean. Nobody gets hurt. That's my condition."
Marcus
"Sure, kid. Nobody gets hurt."

To protect himself, Jonah used Jake's name on some of the paperwork. Jake's contact information. Jake's address. Just a precaution, Jonah told himself. Just a layer of distance in case something ever went wrong. Jake would never know. Nothing would go wrong.

Scene 4 · Route 28 · November 22nd
A Deal Gone Wrong

November 22nd. A deal off Route 28, twenty minutes from campus. The buyer tried to walk away after already taking the product. The situation escalated in under sixty seconds — raised voices, then a shove, then a sound that Jonah would hear for years afterward.

Jonah didn't pull the trigger. But he was there. He stood frozen and watched. And he ran.

Act II — The Confrontation
Scene 5 · SUNY Oneonta · Sophomore Fall
The Name on the List

Jake came back for sophomore year with a new roommate, new ambitions, and zero knowledge of what Jonah had done in his name. As far as Jake knew, his freshman roommate had gotten caught with drugs and left school. Sad, but it happened. College was like that sometimes.

Then, three weeks in, two men appeared at his dorm room door. Big. Quiet in the way that fills a room wrong.

Man at the door
"You Jake Simone? Jonah's contact?"
Jake
"I don't — I barely knew him. I don't know what you're talking about."
Man at the door
"Your name's on paper, son. That makes you part of this whether you like it or not. We'll be back."

They weren't bluffing. Jake knew it immediately. Jonah had used his name — his real name, his real contact details — on deliveries connected to a night where someone died. The people connected to that night were still out there, and they were cleaning up loose ends.

Act II — The Confrontation
Scene 6 · Admin Building · Campus Security Office
Going on Record

Jake reported the visit immediately. Campus security opened a file. They interviewed him — the whole thing recorded, at Jake's request. He wanted it on record that he had no knowledge of any criminal activity, that his name had been used without his consent, and that he had been threatened by men he couldn't identify.

Campus security confirmed no evidence connecting Jake to any crime. Jake had been a victim of identity theft within a criminal operation — nothing more.

But none of that stopped the men from coming back. They showed up again two nights later, standing in the parking lot outside his dorm, just watching. Not doing anything. Just making sure Jake knew they could.

Jake sat on his bed at 2am staring at the ceiling. He had a decision to make.

NODE 2 — What does Jake do?
Act II — Path A: Face to Face
Scene 6B · Off Campus · Three Days Later
The Wrong Move

Jake was not the kind of person who ran from things. He'd grown up playing competitive soccer, arguing cases in his high school debate team, pushing back when pushing back was the right call. So he made a decision that felt brave at the time and stupid almost immediately after: he sent a message through a mutual contact asking to meet with Marcus Webb directly.

They met in the parking lot of a strip mall off Route 23. Marcus arrived alone, which Jake took as a good sign. It wasn't.

Jake
"I don't know anything. I wasn't involved in anything. My name being on that paperwork was Jonah's decision, not mine. I want it removed and I want to be left alone."
Marcus Webb
"I respect that. I do. But here's the thing about paperwork, Jake — it doesn't care about intent. And neither do the people above me who've seen it."
Jake
"Then tell them. Tell them I had nothing to do with it."
Marcus Webb
"I'll pass the message along. In the meantime — you should think seriously about how visible you want to be. On campus. In general. You understand what I mean."

Jake understood exactly what he meant. He drove back to campus, walked into his dorm room, and started packing. The conversation hadn't ended anything. It had made it worse. Now Marcus Webb knew that Jake was willing to make contact — which meant Marcus Webb knew Jake was scared enough to try.

He called his parents that night.

Jake (calling home)
"I need to come home. I made a mistake trying to handle it myself. Please don't tell Tim — he's too young. He doesn't need to know any of this."

Act II — The Confrontation
Scene 7 · Two Weeks After
The Disappearance

Jake Simone withdrew from SUNY Oneonta without finishing the semester. He told almost no one. He transferred credits, re-enrolled at a school closer to home, and rebuilt his life piece by piece — quietly, carefully, always watching over his shoulder a little.

He never talked about what happened. Not to friends, not to professors, not to the police beyond his one official statement. He just became a story people told in whispers: the sophomore who left mid-semester, who'd been caught up in something, who'd walked out of it somehow.

Over time, the story grew. On a campus where gossip moves fast and memory compresses events into myth, Jake Simone became something of a legend.

And four years later, his younger brother arrived to find out why.

Act III — The Resolution
Scene 8 · SUNY Oneonta · Move-In Day · Present
A Familiar Name in a Strange Place

Tim Simone was eighteen years old, a dual Biology and Education major, and absolutely determined to have a normal college experience. He planned to study hard, maybe join a club, eventually become a high school science teacher. He had no interest in drama.

On his very first day, walking across the quad with a box of textbooks, his English professor stopped him cold.

Prof. Hartley
"Simone — Tim Simone. Any relation to Jake Simone? The soccer player?"
Tim
"Yeah — he's my older brother. You knew him?"
Prof. Hartley
"Knew of him. Most people here did, at one point. He's something of a legend. Let's just say he handled himself in a situation most people wouldn't have. Interesting young man."

Tim had been eight years old when Jake left home. He remembered the tension, the packed bag, the too-long hug. He'd been told it was "complicated adult stuff." For ten years, that was all he'd ever gotten.

Now he was walking the same hallways. And the question that had lived in the back of his mind his entire life was suddenly standing right in front of him.

What does Tim do?
Act III — Path A: The Investigation
Scene 9A · Library Archives · Week Two
Pulling at Threads

Tim started small. He pulled campus newspaper archives from four years ago. He searched digitized public records. He found classmates of Jake's who were now seniors and grad students — people who'd heard things, seen things, who remembered that semester.

The deeper Tim dug, the more the picture sharpened. This wasn't a simple drug bust story. Someone had died. The people responsible were still out there. And his brother had walked out of it by the thinnest margin imaginable.

Senior student (who knew Jake)
"I'll tell you what I know — but understand this: your brother made a choice to disappear. That was a smart choice. The kind of smart that keeps you alive."

Tim sat with that for a long time.

Act III — Path A
Scene 10A · Tim's Dorm Room · Late Night
The Call

Tim called Jake at 11pm on a Thursday. Jake picked up on the third ring. There was a long pause after Tim said what he'd found.

Jake
"How much do you know?"
Tim
"Enough. I know about Jonah. I know about the paperwork with your name on it. I know about the shooting."
Jake
"Tim... I left so you wouldn't ever have to think about any of this."
Tim
"You made that choice for me. I'm eighteen. I'm at the same school. I think I get to decide now."

Jake went quiet for a full thirty seconds. When he spoke again, he told Tim everything — the visit, the threats, the security interview, the parking lot, the bag, the drive home. He told Tim what he'd never told anyone.

Jake
"The man who was shot — he has a family. Jonah's in prison partly because of something that happened in my dorm room doorway. It's not a clean story, Tim. There's no ending that makes everyone okay."
Tim's decision — the full truth is now in his hands
Act III — Path B: Looking Away
Scene 9B · Tim's Dorm Room
You Can't Outrun a Story That Lives in the Walls

Tim went back to his dorm, unpacked his books, and told himself what he'd been telling himself for years: Jake left for reasons. Adults have reasons. It's not his place to know.

He lasted four days.

On the fifth day, a lab partner in Biology mentioned hearing the Simone name in connection with something that had happened years ago. Then a resident advisor said the same thing, casually, like it was a campus legend everyone knew. By the end of the week it was undeniable: whether Tim asked questions or not, the story was going to find him.

Tim (to himself)
"You can't outrun something that happened in every hallway you walk through."

He called Jake that night. Jake picked up on the third ring and went silent when Tim said the words "SUNY Oneonta."

Jake
"You're there. Of course you're there. Tim — don't dig into this. I'm serious. It's been years, but some things don't just go away."
Tim
"What happened, Jake? Just tell me."
Jake
"Someone died. That's what happened. I ran so you would never have to think about it. Please — let it stay buried."

Act III — Ending A
Scene 11 · Epilogue · Six Months Later
What We Carry

Tim kept the truth to himself. He filed it somewhere deep — not forgotten, exactly, but contained. He understood now what Jake had done: not run from responsibility, but run toward Tim's safety. Toward the possibility that Tim could walk these same hallways unmarked by what came before.

He threw himself into his classes. Biology labs. Student teaching prep. The ordinary rhythms of a freshman year. He sat in the same dining hall his brother had once sat in. Walked the same paths across the quad.

But the paranoia crept in slowly. Every unfamiliar face in a crowd. Every car that idled too long near the dorms. Tim had buried the truth — but burial isn't the same as gone.

Tim (journal entry, March)
"I understand why Jake left. I understand what he was protecting me from. I just don't know yet if protection and ignorance are the same thing."

He never stopped watching.

Act III — Ending B
Scene 12 · Epilogue · Eight Months Later
Bringing It Into the Light

Tim reported everything — to campus authorities first, then to the Otsego County Sheriff's Office. He gave a full voluntary statement, organized everything he'd found in his research notes, and asked his brother to do the same. Jake, after a long pause on the phone, agreed.

The investigation was reopened. It took months. Jake gave a full deposition — the visit to his dorm, the threats, the parking lot, all of it on record. Every charge against him was formally dropped. He had been a victim, and the record now said so.

Marcus Webb was eventually charged with conspiracy and accessory to manslaughter. Jonah, still serving time, cooperated with prosecutors in exchange for a reduced sentence. He'd been waiting years for someone to ask the right questions.

Jake (phone call, after the charges)
"You could have stayed out of it. I told you to stay out of it. Why didn't you listen?"
Tim
"Because I was already in it. We both were. You just made sure I didn't know."

Tim finished his freshman year with a clearer sense of who his brother was — not a legend, not a ghost, but a person who'd made scared decisions in an impossible situation and somehow survived them. That felt like enough to build on.

Ending B: The Truth Comes Out
Tim chose transparency over safety — and gave everyone involved a chance at something like resolution. Justice came slowly and incompletely. But it came. Sometimes the hardest path is the one that leads somewhere real.
